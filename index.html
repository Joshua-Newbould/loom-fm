A folio on looped mixture-of-experts · MNIST & CIFAR-10

LOOM×H

A small flow-matching transformer is run over its own output several times, with a bank of experts shared across passes. Does looping buy image quality more cheaply than width?

IThe idea

A stack of M transformer blocks is applied H times in a row. Attention weights and experts are shared by every pass; each pass routes tokens through the experts with a router of its own. Compute grows with recurrence, capacity with sparsity, and the parameter count barely moves.

LOOM makes looping stable beyond two passes with a few small devices: residual branches scaled down, the input re-injected at the start of each loop, a moving average of past branch outputs carried across loops and layers, and backpropagation cut into segments, each with its own loss.

γ = λ / (H √M)
h ← h + γ·RMS(MoE(RMS(h)))
h0t = (1 − gt) hMt−1 + gt x

Here the language model becomes a JiT-style denoiser: a patch-2 transformer predicts the clean image from a noisy point on the straight path between Gaussian noise and data, and samples are drawn with fifty Euler steps, class-conditional, without guidance.

IITwo fronts

Each family was trained once at a ladder of widths, with one step budget per dataset, and scored by FID on 10,000 samples; lower is better. A point stays on its front only if no smaller model of the same family scores better. Parameters count every expert, active or not.

At this budget the loop does not pay. On both datasets the dense front sits at or below LOOM at every size, and the gap is widest for the smallest models. A gain seen on CIFAR-10 in shorter iteration runs did not survive the longer sweep.

IVThe recipe

  1. Cut backpropagation after every loop and put a loss on each segment. This was the largest single gain: about 16% lower FID at three loops on CIFAR-10.
  2. CIFAR-10 prefers three loops, eight experts and top-1 routing; MNIST two loops, eight experts and top-2.
  3. Keep λ = β = 0.5, depth four, all five LOOM components on.
  4. Did not help: a separate adaLN per loop, λ of 1.0 or 0.25, β of 0.9. Four loops did not fit the shared GPU’s memory cap.
